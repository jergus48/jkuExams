Where JKU is strong

  • Mathematics (31.5 ECTS: linear algebra, analysis, probability, numerical optimization) and statistics. This is a real advantage over bootcamp people and over self-taught candidates.
  • Classical ML theory: supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement learning, signal processing, NLP basics.
  • Algorithms, data structures, logic, formal models. Useful for interviews and research roles.

Where it leaves you exposed

  • Almost nothing on shipping software: Git workflows, testing, Docker, CI/CD, cloud, APIs, monitoring.
  • Nothing dedicated to LLMs: transformer internals at depth, fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA), RAG, agents, evaluation, inference serving.
  • No SQL/data engineering, pipelines or experiment tracking.
  • Courses like "Responsible AI", "Gender Studies" and "Technology and Society" (about 10.5 ECTS) do not appear in job requirements, apart from EU AI Act awareness being a small plus.

Suggested priority

The job market pays for people who can build, deploy and evaluate, not just train. Order of value: (1) Python engineering + Git + Docker, (2) PyTorch and Hugging Face, (3) RAG and LLM apps with evaluation, (4) fine-tuning and serving, (5) MLOps and cloud, (6) one deep specialty. What moves you up the range

  • Production experience: deployed model serving real users, with monitoring.
  • LLM systems skills (RAG, evaluation, inference optimization) are in current demand.
  • Cloud + Kubernetes + MLOps pairing is repeatedly named as the biggest premium.
  • Location: Munich and Zurich pay the most; Berlin the least of the large hubs.
  • Master's degree is often preferred for research/senior roles in Switzerland (ETH, EPFL ecosystem). Consider JKU's AI master or a stronger master abroad; a portfolio can substitute for some engineering roles but not all.
